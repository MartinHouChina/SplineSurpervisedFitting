\noindent\textit{Notes.}
The cohort contains 61 curves: 21 synthetic curves and 10 curves from each of UJI handwriting, Natural Earth coastline, USGS contours, and industrial offsets.
This is not a 10\% sample of each dataset. Industrial offsets are procedurally generated industrial-model curves.
The maximum internal-knot budget is 32.

Let $e_{s,i}=\|C_s(t_{s,i})-Q_{s,i}\|_2^2$.
For curve $s$, $\mathrm{MSE}_s=N_s^{-1}\sum_i e_{s,i}$ and $\mathrm{MaxSE}_s=\max_i e_{s,i}$.
Mean MSE is the arithmetic mean over finite returned fits, including tolerance violations.
Worst MaxSE is $\max_{s,i}e_{s,i}$ over all finite fits in the reported group, not the average of per-curve maxima.
Errors use normalized coordinates, with no square root or coordinate-dimension division.
A curve passes only if $\mathrm{MSE}_s\leq5\times10^{-5}$ and $\mathrm{MaxSE}_s\leq5\times10^{-4}$.
Pass rates include every requested curve and algorithm failure in the denominator.
Mean $K$ also uses finite returned fits; one Natural Earth fit failed for Dung (9/10 finite, 60/61 overall).

Time is the mean measured total time over requested cases, including the original method and all numerical postprocessing; it is not network-only time.
These are local exploratory single-run timings, not a controlled hardware speedup study.
Historical protocol: all six methods use insertion repair, while only Ours additionally uses redundant-knot post-pruning.
Literature methods are repository adaptations.
Displayed values are preserved from the source report; no aggregate has been recomputed from rounded entries.
